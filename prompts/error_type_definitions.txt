guess: The answer has no sensible link to the question, as if the student just said a number or word without working it out. Example: asked "What is 5 times 2?", answers "5".
careless: The student clearly knows the method but slipped on a small step or fact, so the answer is close to correct. Example: asked "What is 9 times 8?", answers "81".
right-idea: The student is on the right track but the answer is incomplete or not fully worked out. Example: asked how to round 17 to the nearest ten, answers "you turn the 1 into a 2".
misinterpret: The student answered a different question from the one asked, for example giving a number when a formula or definition was asked for. Example: asked "What is the formula for the volume of a rectangular prism?", answers "59".
imprecise: The answer is essentially right but vague, incomplete in wording, or in the wrong form. Example: asked "What is a rectangle?", answers "a shape with 4 sides".
diagnose: It is not possible to tell what the student was thinking: the message is empty, not an attempt, or too unclear to judge. The tutor would first need to find out more.
no_error: The answer is correct.
